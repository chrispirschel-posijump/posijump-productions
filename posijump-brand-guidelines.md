# PosiJump Productions: Brand Guidelines

> A marketing practice with the energy of a punk rock show and the discipline of a touring band.

---

## 1. Brand Foundation

### The Name

**PosiJump Productions.** Named after the punk rock term for when a guitarist leaps mid-riff. One of those moments of pure, unrehearsable energy that turns a song into a show. It's a name built on a belief: the best marketing work carries that same kinetic quality. Strategic underneath. Alive on the surface.

- **Full name:** PosiJump Productions
- **Short form:** PosiJump (acceptable in informal contexts, social handles, URLs)
- **Never:** Posi-Jump, Posi Jump, PJP, or PosiJump Prod.

### The Person

**Chris Pirschel** is the face of PosiJump Productions. The brand is designed to flex, staying solo-friendly today while leaving room to grow into a small studio tomorrow. For now, Chris is front and center. First-person voice is encouraged. "I" and "we" can coexist depending on context: "I" for direct client work and personal POV, "we" when referring to the practice itself or its outputs.

### What PosiJump Does

A full-stack marketing practice for small businesses and solopreneurs. Services include:

- Marketing consulting
- Content strategy
- Copywriting and editing
- SEO
- Podcast production and hosting
- Social media management

The through-line across all of these: **sharp strategy, executed with personality.** Not marketing theater. Not agency bloat. Work that actually moves the needle, delivered by someone who gives a damn.

### The Positioning

> *Marketing work with the energy of a band that actually loves the songs.*

PosiJump Productions exists for business owners who want results *and* want to enjoy the process. The brand is built against two failure modes common in the marketing world:

1. **The corporate-speak shop.** Polished, safe, forgettable. Delivers decks nobody reads.
2. **The "edgy agency" shop.** Performatively quirky, style over substance. Good at branding itself, less good at delivering for clients.

PosiJump threads the needle: **strategic rigor + real personality + DIY ethic.** The punk bands that inspired this brand (New Found Glory, The Starting Line, The Menzingers, Taking Back Sunday, Dashboard Confessional) all shared one thing: they were technically sharp, but they wrote hooks. They wanted you to remember the song. That's the bar for the work.

## 2. Voice & Tone

### Voice (always)

Voice is who PosiJump is, consistently, across every surface.

- **Confident, not smug.** Knows the work, shows it through specificity, not self-congratulation.
- **Direct, not blunt.** Respects the reader's time without being cold.
- **Funny the way a good tour manager is funny.** Dry, observational, no wasted words. Humor is a side effect of honesty, never the goal.
- **Anti-jargon, pro-clarity.** If a term sounds like it came from a LinkedIn thought-leadership post, cut it.
- **Warm.** This is the part the punk framing might obscure, but it's critical. The reference bands are emo-adjacent for a reason: they were loud, but they were earnest. Clients should feel like PosiJump is on their side, not performing at them.

### Tone (situational)

Tone flexes depending on context:

| Context | Tone |
|---|---|
| Website hero / landing pages | Bold, declarative, a little playful |
| Service pages | Clear, confident, benefit-led |
| Case studies | Narrative, specific, results-focused |
| Social posts (LinkedIn) | Professional, insight-forward, occasional humor |
| Social posts (other platforms) | Looser, more personality, more fun |
| Email to prospects | Warm, direct, human |
| Email to clients | Collaborative, clear, accountable |

### Writing Rules

**Do:**
- Use contractions. Always. "It's," "you're," "we're."
- Write in active voice.
- Use specific numbers, names, and examples over vague claims.
- Start sentences with "And" or "But" when it helps the rhythm.
- Use one-sentence paragraphs for emphasis.
- Read it out loud before publishing. If it sounds like a human wrote it, keep it.

**Don't:**
- Use "leverage," "synergy," "ideate," "unlock," "journey," "solution," "seamless," "best-in-class," or "cutting-edge."
- Force the punk theme into copy. The attitude shows in the *approach*, not in constant music references.
- Curse gratuitously. A well-placed "damn" or "hell" is fine and sometimes useful. Anything stronger is almost never worth it.
- Write paragraphs longer than four sentences on the web.
- Use exclamation points except in genuinely celebratory moments.
- Use em dashes (—) anywhere in copy. Replace with a period, comma, colon, or restructure the sentence. This applies to headlines, body copy, metadata, titles, and any reader-facing text.

### Quick Voice Test

Before publishing any piece of copy, ask:

1. Would Chris actually say this out loud to a client?
2. Is there a more specific way to make this point?
3. Is anything here doing work the reader didn't need done?

If any answer is "no," edit.

### Tagline

**Primary tagline:**

> *Bringing catchy hooks and a DIY work ethic to digital marketing.*

This is the line. It does the work of explaining what PosiJump is in one sentence: the hooks (catchy, memorable, strategic) and the DIY work ethic (self-reliant, scrappy, real) are the two themes everything else builds from. Use it:

- Under the logo in the website hero or about section
- In meta descriptions and SEO title tags
- At the top of proposals and pitch decks
- In email signatures
- Anywhere a prospect needs to understand PosiJump in one sentence

Don't shorten it, don't paraphrase it in brand-level materials, and don't swap it out casually. It's the anchor.

## 3. Color Palette

The palette is designed for restraint with moments of loud. Most surfaces should feel grown-up and confident. The accents come in where they matter: CTAs, highlights, punctuation.

### Primary Colors

| Role | Name | Hex | RGB | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anchor | **Stage Black** | `#17181b` | 23, 24, 27 | Primary text, dark backgrounds, logo default |
| Paper | **Liner Note** | `#e8e4dc` | 232, 228, 220 | Primary background, light surfaces |
| Mid | **Concrete** | `#3a3d42` | 58, 61, 66 | Secondary text, dividers, UI chrome |

### Accent Colors

| Role | Name | Hex | RGB | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Voltage | **Highlighter** | `#d9f266` | 217, 242, 102 | Primary accent. CTAs, highlights, emphasis. Use sparingly. It's loud on purpose. |
| Deep | **Cobalt** | `#1e3a8a` | 30, 58, 138 | Secondary accent. Links, specific callouts, supporting emphasis. |

### Optional Extension

| Role | Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deep cut | **Amp Olive** | `#646b3c` | Retained from original palette as an optional fifth color. Use only for specific contexts where a muted, earthy tone is called for (e.g., merch, print zines). Not for primary UI. |

### Palette Rules

1. **80/15/5 rule.** Roughly 80% neutral (Stage Black, Liner Note, Concrete), 15% one accent (Highlighter *or* Cobalt, one per surface), 5% the other accent or white space.
2. **Never put Highlighter on Cobalt or vice versa.** They fight. One accent per composition.
3. **Highlighter on Stage Black is the signature move.** Use it for the loudest moments: hero CTAs, punctuation marks, key stats.
4. **Cobalt is for substance, Highlighter is for energy.** If you're highlighting a testimonial or a data point, use Cobalt. If you're highlighting a call-to-action or a moment of brand personality, use Highlighter.
5. **Never use pure white (`#ffffff`).** Always default to Liner Note (`#e8e4dc`) for light surfaces. Pure white reads sterile; Liner Note reads like paper.
6. **Never use pure black (`#000000`).** Always Stage Black (`#17181b`). Slightly warmer, reads less harsh, feels less corporate.

### Accessibility

- Stage Black on Liner Note: passes AAA for all text sizes.
- Concrete on Liner Note: passes AA for normal text, AAA for large text.
- Highlighter (`#d9f266`) is **not an accessible text color** on light backgrounds. Only use Highlighter as a background fill with Stage Black text on top, or as a large graphic accent. Never as body text color.
- Cobalt on Liner Note: passes AA. Safe for link text.

## 4. Typography

### Type System

Sharp sans-serif + condensed display, clean and modern with room for impact.

| Role | Recommended | Fallback Stack |
|---|---|---|
| **Display** (H1, hero) | **Archivo Black** or **Anton**: condensed, heavy, high-impact | `"Archivo Black", "Anton", "Impact", sans-serif` |
| **Headings** (H2–H4) | **Inter** or **Space Grotesk**: sharp, modern, versatile | `"Inter", "Space Grotesk", system-ui, sans-serif` |
| **Body** | **Inter**: excellent screen legibility | `"Inter", system-ui, -apple-system, sans-serif` |
| **Accent / mono** | **JetBrains Mono** or **IBM Plex Mono**: tags, labels, small UI copy with a DIY/technical feel | `"JetBrains Mono", "IBM Plex Mono", monospace` |

All four are free on Google Fonts.

### Type Scale (web)

Base: 16px. Scale uses a 1.25 ratio with manual adjustments.

| Level | Size | Weight | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display XL | 72px / 4.5rem | 900 | Hero only. One per page. |
| Display L | 56px / 3.5rem | 900 | Major section openers |
| H1 | 40px / 2.5rem | 800 | Page titles |
| H2 | 32px / 2rem | 700 | Section headings |
| H3 | 24px / 1.5rem | 700 | Subsections |
| H4 | 20px / 1.25rem | 600 | Card titles, minor headings |
| Body L | 18px / 1.125rem | 400 | Lead paragraphs |
| Body | 16px / 1rem | 400 | Default |
| Small | 14px / 0.875rem | 400 | Captions, metadata |
| Micro | 12px / 0.75rem | 500 (mono) | Labels, tags |

### Typographic Rules

- **Display type always tight.** Line-height 0.95–1.05. Let big type feel like a shout.
- **Body type always breathable.** Line-height 1.5–1.65. Let reading feel calm.
- **Use monospace as punctuation, not paragraphs.** Section labels, tags, metadata, form field labels: monospace. Never for body copy.
- **Hyphenation off on display type.** On for body on narrow viewports.
- **No text-transform: uppercase on body.** Only on micro labels and tags.
- **One italic per screen.** Italics are for a single emphasized idea, usually a tagline, pull quote, or moment of voice. Never italicize entire paragraphs.

## 5. Logo & Wordmark

### Wordmark Concept

The primary expression of the brand is a **wordmark**, not a pictorial mark. This keeps the identity type-driven, flexible, and cheap to iterate, the DIY way.

**Concept:** "PosiJump" set in **Archivo Black** or **Anton**, all lowercase, set tight. The word reads flat on the baseline. The **"J"** is the signature letter, marked by color alone: Highlighter fill with a Stage Black outline, inline with the rest of the word at rest. When the mark lives in an interactive context, the J jumps. Raised 10–15% above the baseline, tilted 4–7 degrees counterclockwise, with a hard-offset drop shadow. On rest, it's clean type. On hover or focus, the word does what the word means.

```
posijump      ← rest: J sits inline, Highlighter fill with Stage Black outline
posi[J]ump    ← hover/focus: J leaps above the baseline and tilts counterclockwise
      ^
      the gesture is earned, not static
```

**Variations:**

1. **Primary wordmark (interactive surfaces):** `posiJump` with the J as the signature letter. Sits inline at rest, jumps on hover or keyboard focus. The J's color marks it as the mark even when it isn't moving.
2. **Static wordmark (print, social avatars, anywhere without hover):** Same composition, J raised 10–15% and tilted 4–7 degrees counterclockwise by default. Use this for any surface a reader can't interact with.
3. **Full lockup:** `posiJump productions` with "productions" set in smaller monospace (JetBrains Mono, roughly 30% the size of the wordmark), aligned to the baseline of "posiJump".
4. **Stacked lockup:** `posiJump` on top, `productions` below in mono, left-aligned.
5. **Monogram:** Just the **J**, used as favicon, social avatar, or mark-only applications. Always the static tilted version. Can sit inside a simple circle or square container.

### Logo Rules

- **Minimum size:** 80px wide for full wordmark, 24px square for monogram.
- **Clear space:** Always maintain clear space equal to the x-height of the wordmark on all sides. On interactive surfaces, reserve an extra 15% of the x-height above the J so the hover jump doesn't clip.
- **Color applications:**
  - Stage Black wordmark on Liner Note (default).
  - Liner Note wordmark on Stage Black (dark mode).
  - Highlighter J with a Stage Black outline in every application. The J always gets the voltage color. The rest of the letters stay neutral.
  - Never use Cobalt as the wordmark color.
- **Motion rules (interactive only):**
  - Animate only `transform` (translate + rotate) and `filter` (drop-shadow). Never animate color, weight, or size.
  - Duration 320–420ms with a spring curve, for example `cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1)`.
  - Trigger on parent link hover or keyboard focus. Never on page load or scroll.
  - Respect `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`. Disable the jump entirely in that case.
- **Don't:**
  - Rotate the full wordmark. Only the J tilts, and only in the static variation or during the hover gesture.
  - Add drop shadows or glows to the rest state. The hover drop shadow is the only exception.
  - Stretch or compress.
  - Outline any letter other than the J.
  - Place on busy photographic backgrounds without a solid fill behind it.

### Tone of the Mark

The mark has two registers: **the rest** and **the leap**.

The rest is clean, declarative, confident. Flat type on a baseline with one letter marked by color. Nothing performing.

The leap is earned. It happens when the reader reaches for it (a hover, a focus, a print surface where the static tilt carries the energy). When it lands, the letter should feel like it actually leapt, not like it was offset. The tilt reinforces motion, not decoration. Test both states: the rest should hold up as a confident wordmark on its own, and the leap should feel inevitable, not gimmicky.

## 6. Visual Language

### Design Principles

1. **Generous white space.** Let the content breathe. Crowding signals desperation.
2. **High contrast, low clutter.** One focal point per section. One accent color per composition.
3. **Asymmetric layouts.** Perfect symmetry reads corporate. Slight off-balance reads alive.
4. **Type as image.** Big, expressive typography is the primary visual device. Photography and illustration are secondary.
5. **Texture sparingly.** A subtle paper grain on backgrounds or a slight halftone on accent blocks can reinforce the zine/DIY heritage without going full punk-flyer.

### Photography Direction

- **Candid over posed.** Environmental shots over studio headshots.
- **Warm, natural light.** Avoid cold office lighting or over-corrected skin tones.
- **Chris's headshot should look like Chris.** Not a glamour shot. Not a "professional headshot" with a gray backdrop. Something with character.
- **Texture is good.** Concrete, wood, paper, handwritten notes, a desk with actual things on it.

### Illustration / Graphic Elements

- **Allowed:** Simple geometric accents, hand-drawn arrows or underlines, halftone patterns, bold icon sets (Phosphor Icons or Lucide work well, both free).
- **Not allowed:** Stock illustrations of people, corporate abstract shapes, isometric illustrations, gradient-heavy graphics.

### The Jump

Whenever possible, incorporate upward motion into the design. A rising arrow. A stat that jumps off the baseline. A CTA that sits slightly higher than expected. This is the visual signature: motion, not just color.

## 7. Application Examples

### Website Hero (structure, not copy)

```
[ Small monospace label: "MARKETING / CONTENT / SEO / PODCASTING" ]

[ Display XL headline, Stage Black on Liner Note:
  "Marketing that actually [J]umps." ]
  (The J in "Jumps" is the signature letter: Highlighter fill with a Stage Black outline. Inline at rest, jumps on hover.)

[ Body L subhead, Concrete:
  "Sharp strategy, real personality, and the DIY ethic small
  businesses actually need. I'm Chris Pirschel, and this is
  PosiJump Productions." ]

[ CTA button: Highlighter background, Stage Black text:
  "Let's talk →" ]
```

### Section Header Pattern

- Monospace micro-label in Concrete ("01 / SERVICES")
- Display L headline in Stage Black
- One-line Body L subhead in Concrete
- Content below

### Callout / Pull Quote Pattern

- Large Body L text in Stage Black
- Left border: 4px solid Highlighter
- Generous padding
- Attribution in monospace, Concrete, below

### CTA Patterns

- **Primary CTA:** Highlighter fill, Stage Black text, no border, slight hover-lift.
- **Secondary CTA:** No fill, Stage Black text, Stage Black underline, Highlighter underline on hover.
- **Tertiary (inline link):** Cobalt text, no underline by default, underline on hover.

A quick reference for the gut check.

| PosiJump IS | PosiJump ISN'T |
|---|---|
| A marketing practice with a point of view | An agency trying to be everything to everyone |
| Strategic and fun | Serious and boring, or fun without substance |
